Perpetra charges two kinds of fees: **trading fees**, applied when you open or close a position, and **liquidation fees**, applied when a position is liquidated. Both are set per-market and both flow through the same contract, `FeeCollector`.

<Note>
  Funding is not a fee. Funding payments move directly between longs and shorts and never touch `FeeCollector`. See [Funding Rate](/trading/funding-rate) for a full explanation.
</Note>

## Trading Fees

Every order pays either a **taker fee** or a **maker fee**, depending on whether it removed liquidity from the order book or added it:

| Order Type   | Fee Applied |
|---|---|
| Market order | Taker fee   |
| Limit order  | Maker fee   |

Maker fees are always less than or equal to taker fees. The protocol enforces this at configuration time, since makers provide liquidity to the book and are rewarded with a lower rate for doing so.

Each market publishes its own rates, expressed in basis points (1 bps = 0.01%):

```solidity
struct MarketFeeConfig {
    bool    exists;
    uint256 takerFeeBps;
    uint256 makerFeeBps;
    uint256 liquidationFeeBps;
}
```

### Fee Formula

The fee is a flat percentage of the order's notional size in USD:

```
fee = (sizeUsd * feeBps) / 10_000
```

**Worked example** — opening a $10,000 position on a market with a 5 bps taker fee:

```
fee = (10,000 * 5) / 10,000 = $5
```

Perpetra's Engine calculates this via `calcTradingFee` before settlement and deducts the amount from your collateral as part of opening or closing the trade.

## Liquidation Fees

When a position is liquidated, a fee is taken from the position's remaining collateral and paid directly to the **keeper** who triggered the liquidation:

```
liquidationFee = (sizeUsd * liquidationFeeBps) / 10_000
```

This payment is the keeper's incentive to monitor underwater positions and liquidate them promptly. It is credited directly to the keeper's account — it does not enter the general fee pool or affect the trading-fee distribution described below.

For cross-margin liquidations that close several positions in a single batch, a single aggregate liquidation fee is paid to the keeper, with every closed position recorded against that one payment for accurate indexing.

<Note>
  The liquidation fee is deducted from your remaining collateral at the time of liquidation. It does not add any extra cost beyond that deduction — it is simply the portion of what remains that goes to the keeper rather than back to you.
</Note>

## Where Trading Fees Go

Taker and maker trading fees accumulate inside `FeeCollector` and are periodically distributed to two destinations at a configurable ratio that defaults to:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Insurance Fund" icon="shield">
    Receives **20%** of distributed trading fees. Provides a backstop for the protocol in edge-case insolvency scenarios.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Treasury" icon="building-columns">
    Receives **80%** of distributed trading fees. Funds ongoing protocol operations and development.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The on-chain calculation is:

```
insuranceAmount = (pending * insuranceFundBps) / 10_000
treasuryAmount  = pending - insuranceAmount
```

Distribution can be triggered by anyone — there is no access restriction on the `distribute()` call, because the destination addresses (Insurance Fund and Treasury) are fixed by the protocol and cannot be changed by the caller. In practice, a keeper runs this on a regular schedule.

Liquidation fees bypass this split entirely and go straight to the keeper who performed the liquidation.

## Checking Fees Before You Trade

Two read-only helpers let you preview fee math without submitting a transaction:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="getMarketFeeConfig(marketId)">
    Returns the current taker fee, maker fee, and liquidation fee rates (all in basis points) for the specified market. Use this to confirm the exact rates before placing a large order.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="previewDistribution(token)">
    Returns what the next `distribute()` call would send to the Insurance Fund versus the Treasury, based on fees currently pending in `FeeCollector` for a given token. Useful for monitoring accrued fees before distribution runs.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
